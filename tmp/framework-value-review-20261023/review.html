Sample size and significance

A 24-task pilot across five arms means 120 arm runs, solely to verify the harness, receipts and task usability. Pilot outcomes are excluded from the main study. Freeze the main corpus, thresholds, comparisons and analysis before running it.

Planning below assumes paired binary success, 80% power, one-sided family-wise alpha 5% over four prespecified contrasts (Bonferroni alpha 0.0125 each), and independent task units. Approximation: n ≈ (z(1−alpha)+z(power))² × paired-discordance / margin². This is a planning approximation, not an exact power guarantee.

A strict 5-percentage-point quality margin at 20% discordance needs approximately 761 distinct tasks: budget roughly 800 tasks / 4,000 main arm runs, plus pilot and internal calls. A 200-task / 1,000-arm-run study can target roughly 10-point differences under this assumption; it must not be advertised as equivalent 5-point assurance. Repeated seeds are stability checks, not extra independent tasks. If adequate genuine cases or an affordable budget are unavailable, narrow the scope or report uncertainty; do not manufacture variants to inflate N.

The analyst must preregister the final paired confidence method and multiplicity handling. A blinded nuisance-rate sizing rule can be fixed in advance; repeated unadjusted peeking or tuning to winning arms is not acceptable. Do not stop early for apparent efficacy without a valid prespecified sequential method.

Proposed go / no-go rules

These are reviewable defaults, not approved thresholds. Let delta-success be candidate minus comparator and let R be total paid-cost-proxy per verified success divided by the comparator. Apply the same predeclared decision rules separately to C versus A, C versus B, D versus C, and E versus D. Report a decision for each comparison; do not force a single overall ranking when quality, cost and latency trade off. A and B are standalone baselines; this four-contrast design does not claim a separate formal A-versus-B superiority test. Use model-specific, versioned receipt/list-price equivalents where invoices are absent, and label actual billed cost unknown.

OutcomeRule
GOMultiplicity-adjusted lower confidence bound for delta-success above −5 pp; upper confidence bound for R below 0.80 (at least 20% material improvement); p95 end-to-end latency within the predeclared task-family SLA and proposed 20% regression cap; at least 95% usable accounting/quality coverage; no added observed critical failures. Report rare-failure bounds.
NO-GOClear evidence the quality loss exceeds the margin (upper bound below −5 pp), a critical safety failure under the predefined rule, or clear evidence the cost gain cannot meet the material bar. Practical rollout can also be declined when verification effort cannot plausibly pay back at measured usage.
INCONCLUSIVEIntervals cross a decision threshold, evidence is missing, a route cannot be run faithfully, or the budget cannot support the promised precision. Failure to prove non-inferiority is not proof of inferiority.

With zero critical events, a rough 95% upper bound is 3/N: zero events in 100 tasks still permits about a 3% true rate. A claim of zero risk is not supported.

Cache, timing and cost controls

  • Use fresh independent task contexts, identical source and acceptance criteria, fixed model/settings/tool envelopes, and balanced randomised arm order.
  • Keep the realistic mixture of cache hits and misses visible. Record cache-write/read/ordinary input/output buckets separately by provider. No unreported warming calls or shared answer/result cache between arms.
  • Queue, setup, context preparation, model load, orchestration, verification and repair are included or explicitly marked unmeasured. Track owned GPU/VRAM where actually observed; no invented energy or contention numbers.
  • Use per-run identities and measured receipts, not whole-session totals. Confidence intervals resample independent tasks and preserve pairing; family weights remain fixed.
  • Freeze any Claude prompt-prefix change before the main comparison. Do not mix prompt optimisation with a halfway router/model change.

V5 preparation boundary and execution readiness

The user-selected direction is a separate token-aware v5 profile with context 8192 and output reserve 192 unchanged, preserving request/source text and the v4 rubric/gates. A separate guard and client/runner path must bind the actual payload, model digest, tokenizer, full chat template/special-token handling and parity evidence, reject stale or mismatched counts before send, and default disabled. An injected counter interface tested with fake counts alone is not a proven runtime fix.

No supported generic Codex worker route or pinned Codex model has yet been established for this study. Existing Bridge analysis cannot edit files or run repository tests. Direct isolated preparation requires the approval demanded by the user's routing instructions. Provider/model runs additionally need approved per-stage call/token/time/money caps and the required adapters/identities. Gemma v5 cannot enter the main arm until tokenizer/template parity is established and pinned. No automatic deployment or changes to the frozen v4 window, original shared quotas or real routing are part of this proposal.
